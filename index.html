German Research Allowance Start-Up Pilot

We are interested in learning what you expect the effects of being offered the pre-filled draft are for these firms. Your answers will be pre-registered and reported alongside the results as one of several priors, following the Bayesian impact evaluation approach of Iacovone, McKenzie, and Meager (Econometrica, 2025). Individual responses are never reported separately.

Background on the research allowance. The research allowance is Germany's R&D tax credit, introduced on 1 January 2020 under the Research Allowance Act (FZulG). Unlike the project-based grant programmes that dominate German innovation policy, it is volume-based and non-selective. Any firm carrying out qualifying R&D is entitled to the credit, irrespective of sector, size, or profitability. The credit is worth 25 percent of eligible R&D personnel and contract-R&D costs, rising to 35 percent for SMEs, and loss-making firms receive it as a cash payout rather than an offset against tax owed.

Background on the pilot. 500 eligible German start-ups have been randomly assigned to two groups of 250. Firms were identified from web-based classification of R&D activity and are contacted through LinkedIn outreach carrying partner endorsement from start up ecosystem organisations.
