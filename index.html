German Research Allowance Start-Up Pilot

We are interested in learning what you expect the effects of being offered the pre-filled draft are for these firms. Your answers will be pre-registered and reported alongside the results as one of several priors, following the Bayesian impact evaluation approach of Iacovone, McKenzie, and Meager (Econometrica, 2025). Individual responses are never reported separately.

Background on the research allowance. The research allowance is Germany's R&D tax credit, introduced on 1 January 2020 under the Research Allowance Act (FZulG). Unlike the project-based grant programmes that dominate German innovation policy, it is volume-based and non-selective. Any firm carrying out qualifying R&D is entitled to the credit, irrespective of sector, size, or profitability. The credit is worth 25 percent of eligible R&D personnel and contract-R&D costs, rising to 35 percent for SMEs, and loss-making firms receive it as a cash payout rather than an offset against tax owed. This last feature makes the instrument unusually valuable to young companies, which typically have R&D payroll but no taxable profit. Total annual volume is approximately €4 billion following three expansions of the scheme since 2020.

Claiming the credit requires two steps. The firm first submits a project description to the Certification Body for the Research Allowance (BSFZ), which certifies whether the work qualifies as R&D under Frascati criteria of novelty, technical uncertainty, and systematic method. Only after certification can the firm claim the credit from its tax office alongside the annual return. Certification is not a formality, roughly 20 to 25% of claimed expenditure is rejected at this stage. Writing a first project description that will survive review is the main fixed cost of entry, and the main thing specialised consultancies are paid to do.

Take-up remains well below the eligible population, and start-ups are the most under represented group, despite being the firms for which the cash payout feature is worth most. The working hypothesis of this study is that the binding constraint is not the size of the subsidy but the fixed cost of producing a credible first draft.
Background on the pilot. 500 eligible German start-ups have been randomly assigned to two groups of 250. Firms were identified from web-based classification of R&D activity and are contacted through LinkedIn outreach carrying partner endorsement from start up ecosystem organisations.

The 250 firms in the control group receive a standard outreach message with a link to the official BSFZ information page, and nothing else.

The 250 firms in the treatment group receive the same message, plus a button offering a pre-filled draft project description. If a founder clicks the button, a draft is generated for that firm from publicly available information about its work and returned to them, they can then edit it, discard it, or submit it. A draft is written only for firms that click. Firms that do not click receive nothing beyond the standard message.

Neither group receives money, consulting hours, or any fee waiver, and neither receives privileged treatment at BSFZ. Firms remain fully responsible for the content of anything they submit. The only difference between the groups is whether the message carries the option to have a first draft written.
How to answer

Allocating your 20 stones

We are interested in learning what you expect the effects of being offered the pre-filled draft are for these firms. For each of the questions, imagine you have 20 stones to allocate to different intervals, based on the probability that you think a specific event will happen. One stone represents 5 percent, or 1 chance out of 20.

If you do not put any stones in a particular range, it means you are sure that this event will NOT happen. As you add stones, it means you think the likelihood the event happens is increasingly likely. If you put two stones in a range, it means you think there is a 10 percent chance of this outcome happening. If you put 10 stones in a range, it means that it is just as likely to happen as not happen, fifty fifty, and if you put all 20 stones in a range, it means you are certain this will be the outcome. There is not a right or wrong answer, we just want to know what you think.

Beliefs about the impact of the offer, Step 4 of 9

Beliefs about the impact of the offer

We are now going to ask for your beliefs about the likely impact of the offer for the firms in the treatment group, compared to the firms in the control group. In technical terms, we want to know your beliefs about the intention-to-treat effect (ITT). This is the effect of BEING OFFERED the draft, regardless of whether or not a firm clicks the button or uses what it receives. It is simply the DIFFERENCE IN MEAN OUTCOMES for the 250 firms whose message carries the offer, compared to the 250 firms whose message does not.

Worked example. Suppose the 250 firms in the treatment group break down as follows.
  • 160 firms never open the message. 4 percent of them apply anyway, as they would have without any contact: 6.4 applications
  • 40 firms open the message but do not click the button. 5 percent of them apply: 2 applications
  • 30 firms click, receive a draft, and do nothing further with it. 7 percent of them apply: 2.1 applications
  • 20 firms click, receive a draft, and work with it. 60 percent of them apply: 12 applications
The application rate for the treatment group is then (6.4 + 2 + 2.1 + 12) / 250 = 9.0 percent.

Suppose the 250 firms in the control group apply at a rate of 4 percent.

The intention-to-treat effect is the difference between these two groups: 9.0 − 4.0 = +5.0 percentage points.

Note that the ITT is diluted by every firm that ignores the message, and that a large effect on the founders who actually engage can still produce a small ITT. We are asking about the ITT throughout, not about the effect on those who engage.

The scales in the questions that follow begin at zero. Put your stones in the 0 box to the extent that you think the offer will make no difference at all, or would make things worse.
